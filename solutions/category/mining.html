Deterministic Multi-Agent Architecture

Open-pit surface mines, deep underground coal seams, and offshore subsea drilling platforms are among the harshest operating environments on earth. Equipment breakdowns, haul truck traffic collisions, geotechnical bench collapses, and deepwater blowout preventer leaks result in catastrophic human casualties and hundreds of millions of dollars in downtime.

Elesium engineers robust, industrial-hardened AI state machines that operate over private LTE/5G wireless mesh networks on heavy extraction sites. Our Autonomous Haulage Dispatch Agent optimizes the routing of 240-ton dump trucks between excavators, in-pit crushers, and waste dumps, balancing shovel queue times and crusher feed rates in sub-second cycles.

For geotechnical safety, our Slope Stability Agent ingests continuous radar interferometry and borehole extensometer streams, detecting sub-millimeter bench movements and predicting catastrophic slope failures hours before rock displacement occurs. In subsea oil and gas engineering, our Wellhead Agent monitors multiplex electro-hydraulic control pod pressure decay curves, identifying seal erosion and valve drift before subsea intervention costs escalate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the system operate in remote mining sites with zero public internet connectivity?
All agents execute entirely on local, ruggedized server racks deployed in site dispatch offices, communicating with vehicles via private LTE, Wi-Fi mesh, or VHF radio data links.
How does Elesium comply with DGMS safety regulations for tele-remote equipment?
Our anti-collision and vehicle dispatch agents maintain hard safety interlocks with redundant fail-safe heartbeat timers that immediately command vehicle emergency stops if telemetry links drop.
Can the software interface with fleet management systems from Caterpillar (MineStar) or Komatsu (Modular)?
Yes. We interface with legacy fleet management systems through certified API gateways and direct database read-replicas without replacing existing on-board hardware.
What sensors are used for geotechnical slope monitoring?
We ingest real-time data from ground-based synthetic aperture radar (GB-SAR), robotic total stations, piezometers, and borehole extensometers.
What is the expected production uplift from automated haulage optimization?
Large open-pit operations typically see a 15% to 22% increase in effective truck-shovel utilization, translating to tens of thousands of additional tons moved per shift.
