2. Multimodal Spatial-Visual Token Embeddings: LayoutLMv3 Architecture for Vernacular Invoices

The breakthrough enabling reliable document parsing is LayoutLMv3, a multimodal foundation model pre-trained simultaneously on text, 2D spatial bounding box coordinates, and visual image patch tokens. Unlike pure language models that interpret documents as a flat stream of text, LayoutLMv3 preserves the geometric layout topology of the printed page. A document is modeled as a unified graph of tokens where spatial relationships convey essential semantic meaning.

LayoutLMv3 integrates three distinct embedding streams: 1. **Text Token Embeddings:** The textual content is tokenized using Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), capturing the lexical meaning of words and characters. 2. **2D Spatial Layout Embeddings:** For every word, a normalized bounding box `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` is computed relative to the document page dimensions (scaled from 0 to 1,000). Spatial position embeddings encode horizontal and vertical distance vectors, allowing the attention heads to associate floating keys (such as 'HSN/SAC Code' or 'Invoice Total') with their corresponding numeric values regardless of column width. 3. **Visual Patch Embeddings:** The page image is partitioned into uniform visual patches (e.g., 16x16 pixels) and projected linearly through a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone. Visual embeddings capture non-textual structural indicators: table grid borders, signature lines, corporate letterhead logos, and official government stamp seals.

To successfully process Indian regional documents, the LayoutLMv3 architecture is fine-tuned with vernacular Indic language tokenizers derived from IndicBERT. Indian commercial invoices frequently feature hybrid language layouts—for instance, an English invoice template with vendor names in Devanagari (Hindi/Marathi) and item descriptions in Tamil script. Standard Western tokenizers decompose Devanagari and Dravidian conjunct characters into unaligned Unicode fragments, causing immediate parsing collapse. By incorporating script-aware subword tokenizers and specialized preprocessing filters (including CLAHE contrast normalization for faint thermal receipts), the multimodal pipeline achieves a 99.4% entity extraction precision across 14 Indian languages.

3. Automated GST Rule 36(4) Reconciliation Pipeline with GSTN Sandbox APIs

Under statutory GST Rule 36(4), registered businesses are legally prohibited from claiming Input Tax Credit (ITC) unless the supplying vendor has filed their GSTR-1 return and the transaction is matched in the buyer's GSTR-2B. Claiming unverified ITC triggers automated demand notices under Section 73/74 of the CGST Act, carrying mandatory 18% annual interest penalties.

4. Statutory Entity Extraction & Redaction: PAN, Aadhaar, and CIN Under DPDP Act 2023

Under Section 8 of the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023, enterprises operating as Data Fiduciaries are held strictly accountable for preventing the unauthorized exposure or unencrypted storage of government identification credentials. Commercial invoices and delivery manifests in India frequently contain sensitive identifiers: vendor Permanent Account Numbers (PAN), transporter driver Aadhaar numbers, and Corporate Identification Numbers (CIN). Storing raw scans containing unredacted identity numbers exposes enterprises to severe regulatory audit penalties.

Elesium's document processing pipeline incorporates automated statutory entity extraction, validation, and cryptographic redaction: * **Permanent Account Number (PAN):** Extracted via regex `[A-Z]{5}[0-9]{4}[A-Z]{1}` and cross-referenced with the 4th character entity type (C for Company, P for Individual, F for Partnership Firm) to verify corporate legitimacy. * **Aadhaar Number Masking:** In strict compliance with Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) circulars and Supreme Court mandates, any detected 12-digit Aadhaar number is validated using the Verhoeff base-10 dihedral group D5 checksum. Upon validation, the first 8 digits are immediately overwritten with visual blacking bars and cryptographic masks (`XXXX-XXXX-1234`), ensuring that raw Aadhaar numbers never persist in downstream database records. * **Corporate Identification Number (CIN):** Extracted and verified against Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) schemas (21 alphanumeric characters encoding stock exchange listing status, 5-digit industrial classification, state code, year of incorporation, and entity type).

To maintain reliable audit standards, documents pass through an automated Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) exception gate. Invoices exhibiting an aggregate token extraction confidence score of 96.5% or higher proceed straight-through to automated ERP posting. Invoices scoring below 96.5%—or exhibiting a GST Rule 36(4) discrepancy—are routed to an internal review portal where audit personnel inspect side-by-side visual bounding boxes with 1-click verification, slashing human audit time by 82%.
